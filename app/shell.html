Sections
Paper folding level
Standard matches the GAN practice test: half folds, diagonal folds and small corner folds, 1–3 folds. Hard and Expert use the same kinds of folds but more of them (up to 4), more holes and closer look-alike answers. Box (cube) folding is not on the paper folding test; it has its own optional drill above.
Mechanical level
Hard gives every question its trickiest version with no hints. Expert adds multi-step comparisons: longer gear trains with idlers, levers loaded away from the end, presses and springs built from several parts, and look-alike setups that turn out to be equal. Nothing in it needs numbers or arithmetic. Applies to the mechanical section and the concepts drill.

The rules that answer most questions
Gears
Meshed gears turn opposite ways. Count the chain: odd number of gears, the last turns like the first. Three gears touching in a loop lock up.
Gear speed
The smaller gear (fewer teeth) turns faster; the bigger gear turns slower. An idler changes direction, never speed.
Belts
Open belt keeps the direction; crossed belt reverses it. The smaller pulley turns faster.
Pulleys
Count the rope segments holding up the moving block. More segments: less force, but more rope to pull.
Levers
The closer the pivot is to the load (and the farther the effort is from the pivot), the less effort you need.
Ramps
A gentler (less steep) ramp takes less force. A longer ramp to the same height is gentler.
Hydraulics
Pressure is equal everywhere. The wider the big piston is compared with the small one, the more the press multiplies your push.
Springs
End to end (series): stretches add. Side by side (parallel): they share the load.
Water pressure
Depends only on depth below the surface, not on tank shape or width.
Spinning wheels
Same turns per minute everywhere, but points farther out move faster.
Tipping
An object falls when its center of gravity passes over its edge. Low and wide is stable.
Circuits
Current needs a closed loop. Series: one open switch kills everything. Parallel: an open switch kills only its own branch.
Cables
A cable can only pull. Put it where the load stretches it: under a bridge deck, from the top hinge corner to the low free corner of a gate. A stiff brace goes the other way.
Cable angles
Flatter cables and slings pull much harder for the same load. Guy wires work best attached at the top and anchored far out.
Hanging chains
The higher hook holds more of the chain's weight.
Leverage
A chain or brace holds best far from the pivot and close to a right angle.
Rolling gears
A gear between a moving rack and a fixed rack moves its center half as far as the rack.
Bikes & winches
Big front / small rear sprocket = hard but fast. Bigger winch drum or shorter handle = harder to turn.
Movable pulley
A pulley riding on the load halves the force and doubles the rope you must pull.
Flywheels
More mass at the rim = harder to start, harder to stop, spins longer.
Motion
Things keep going straight. A skidding car slides to the outside of the curve; a released ball flies off along the tangent.
Water
A jet rises only to the level of its source. Tank width doesn't matter.
Pumps
Both valves in a lift pump open upward. A gear pump carries fluid around the outside of the gears.
Linkages
If the driven wheel's pin is farther from its center than the driving pin, it rocks back and forth instead of turning all the way.
Stepped pulleys
Work one belt at a time. Small driving big slows down; big driving small speeds up.
Wheelbarrows
The load closer to the wheel is easier to lift.
Capacity marks
Marks bunch up where the container is wider; straight walls get even marks.
Cooling
More surface area (fins, thin flat shapes) = cools or melts faster. A ball holds heat longest.
Jets & taps
Deeper holes spurt harder. Connected columns share one water level.
Balance
A weighted disk stops heavy side down. A steelyard: box weight × its distance = counterweight × its distance.
Breaking
A stick levered over a support bends most (and breaks) right over the support.
Thrown balls
Lower on the path = faster. Same height = same speed.
Curves
The outer rail is set higher so the track leans into the turn.
Mixing & ice
Fine stuff fills the air gaps in coarse stuff, so the level drops. Ice takes a little% more room than water; floating ice melts with no level change.
One-tooth wheel
A driver with a single tooth moves the gear in stop-and-go steps.
Ring gears
A gear meshing inside a ring turns the same way as the ring. Only outside-to-outside meshes reverse.
Chains & arms
On an arm pinned to a post, a chain works from above to the loaded end or from below to the other end.
Ratchets
The catch rides up the gentle slope of each tooth and jams against the steep face.
Floating
Floats higher = heavier liquid. Same water, sits deeper = heavier block. Oil floats on water.
Siphons
Faster when the outlet is farther below the water surface it drains.
Inertia
A hanging ball leans back when a vehicle speeds up and forward when it brakes. Braking loads the front wheels.
Supports
The support closer to the load (or to the middle of a plank) carries more.
Bevel gears
Where two bevel gears touch, their teeth move the same way. The back of a turning disk moves opposite to its front.
Falling
Dropped and thrown-sideways balls from the same height land at the same time. Things dropped from a moving plane keep moving forward. Open parachutes fall at a steady speed.
Stability
Low and wide is steady: crouching, drooping balance poles, short wide blocks on slopes. A crane with a flatter boom tips more easily.
Strength
A board on edge is much stiffer than the same board flat. Deeper trusses are stronger. Dams arch toward the water. More umbrella ribs, less flipping.
Flow & pressure
Pressure depends only on depth, not jar shape. Lower hydrants spray harder. A pipe twice as wide carries four times as much. Closed jars need air in to drain.
Heat
Hot water rises, cool water sinks. Dark surfaces get hotter in the sun. Thick glass cracks more easily with boiling water. Cold bottles collect droplets. Water boils cooler up a mountain.
Steering
A boat turns toward the side the rudder’s back edge points. Backing a trailer: turn the wheels opposite to where the trailer should go. Swivel wheels trail behind.
Sound & light
Smaller, shorter, tighter = higher note. Tunnels make sounds louder. Objects under water look higher than they are, so aim below.
Wires
Longer or thinner wire resists more, so less current flows.
Cube patterns
Faces opposite each other on the box can never be seen together. Faces around a corner come in one order only; the mirror order is impossible.
Paper folding
Unfold last fold first. Mirror every hole across each crease. Each fold doubles the holes: 1 punch, 2 folds = 4 holes. Exception: if a narrow strip is folded over, only holes punched through that strip get a mirror copy.

How to work each kind of question
Number series
Write the gaps between numbers under the series. If the gaps don’t make a pattern, try “times something, then plus/minus something”, or two series taking turns (every other number).
Plugging in
Put the given numbers in first, then solve. With answer choices, you can also try each choice and see which makes both sides equal.
Simplify & expand
Multiply the number outside the brackets by every term inside, then combine like terms (x³ with x³, x² with x²). Watch minus signs in front of brackets.
Factoring
For x² + bx + c, find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. For a common factor, take out the biggest number and the lowest power of each letter. Multiply back to check.
Which statement is true
Find where each letter crosses zero (set the other to 0). Decide if they move the same way (positive slope) or opposite ways. A statement is only true if it works for every value in its range; partly-true ones are traps.
Same relationship
Turn everything into y = mx + b, then test two points (x = 0 and one more) in every choice.
Graphs
b is where the line crosses the y-axis. Slope = rise over run: up when positive, down when negative.
Isolate a variable
Undo things in reverse order: move added terms first, then multiply/divide, then powers (square root last).
Proportions
Cross-multiply, then solve. Systems: put each choice into both equations.
